Experiment Trajectory

H3 points fall well above the H2 curve at the same CFG values — latent-8 is strictly worse.

Why Did H3 Fail?

The diffusion denoiser (fixed transformer architecture) must model the latent distribution. With latent-8, it has to model twice as many dimensions — a harder task with the same model capacity. The denoiser is the bottleneck, not the VAE.
Crucially, R-prec@1 barely changed (H3=0.676 vs H2=0.671 at CFG=5). This reveals that the conditioning quality bottleneck is the ego encoder architecture — specifically, mean-pooling 196 timesteps into a single token — not the latent dimension.
H3 diversity was marginally higher (5.842 vs 5.779), consistent with the larger latent space capturing more variety. But the FID cost (+14.5%) far outweighs this tiny diversity gain.

🟡 Next: H4 — Cross-Attention Ego Encoder

Hypothesis

Replace EgoEncoderPooled (mean-pool 196 timesteps → 1 token) with full cross-attention conditioning (trans_dec arch, all 196 timesteps as key/value). The denoiser gets full temporal ego context at each denoising step instead of a compressed summary.

Why This Should Work

  • H3 proved R-prec is decoupled from latent dim — the bottleneck is the ego encoder, not the latent space
  • Mean pooling destroys temporal structure (speed profiles, acceleration, approach angles)
  • Cross-attention allows the model to attend to specific ego timesteps relevant to each latent token
  • Literature: WoSAD (2024) uses cross-attention for polyline conditioning with strong results

Prediction

  • R-prec@1 ↑: expected significant improvement (current 0.671 → target >0.700 at CFG=5)
  • FID: uncertain — may improve if temporal conditioning helps generate more realistic motion; may be neutral if FID is dominated by other factors
  • Diversity: likely stable (same latent-4 VAE)

Implementation Plan

  1. Pre-train EgoEncoder (no pooling, full sequence T=196) with contrastive loss — ~1 day on helma
  2. Train MLD diffusion with arch=trans_dec, passing full ego sequence as K/V — ~2 days on helma (latent-4, same as H2)
  3. Eval at H2-comparable epoch with CFG=5 and CFG=7
The pretrained ego encoder weights from H2 (EgoEncoderPooled) cannot be reused for H4 — the output shape differs (B,1,D) vs (B,T,D). H4 requires fresh encoder pretraining.

Key Learnings

  1. FID is highly sensitive to CFG (H5, confirmed): 10.8% improvement just from CFG=5 vs CFG=15. Best operating points: CFG=5 (FID), CFG=7 (balanced).
  2. Latent dimension is not the bottleneck (H3, confirmed): Same-capacity denoiser performs worse with larger latent. Don't increase latent dim without scaling denoiser.
  3. R-prec is controlled by ego encoder, not latent dim (H3 insight): Mean-pooling to a single token is the likely bottleneck for conditioning quality.
  4. FID doesn't monotonically improve with epochs (H2, confirmed): Best checkpoint ≠ final checkpoint. Periodic FID validation and checkpoint selection critical.
  5. Interaction crop + weighted sampling (H2): Focusing training on ego-ped interaction windows gives FID=6.603 — best result so far.
