Key Observations from Seg-1

FID degrades as R@1 improves — a tradeoff, not a joint improvement

From ep=2099 to ep=3299: FID worsens from 4.952 → 5.311 (+7.3%) while R@1 improves from 0.263 → 0.348 (+32.3%). The model is learning to condition on ego more accurately, but at the cost of generation quality. This is a stronger version of the H4 dynamic — except H6 starts from a worse FID baseline.

MultiModality collapse: MM=2.5 vs H4=4.0

With FREEZE_EGO=False, the ego encoder over-specializes to individual ego trajectories. The denoiser learns to follow the encoder output deterministically, producing near-identical samples for the same condition. This is the opposite of what was hoped — unfreezing trades diversity for conditioning accuracy.

R@1 is still climbing steeply at ep=3299 (0.348, up from 0.273 at ep=2099)

+28% R@1 improvement between ep=2099 and ep=3299 with no sign of plateau. The peak has not been reached. The critical question for seg-2: where does R@1 plateau, and does it ever reach H4's 0.548?

Diversity slightly higher than H4 (6.034 vs 5.794)

Despite MM collapse, the raw motion Diversity metric is marginally better — likely because the unfrozen encoder produces more varied K/V keys for cross-attention, even if per-condition samples are similar to each other.

What MultiModality Measures

MM measures the diversity of outputs given the same conditioning input. High MM = model generates varied motions for one ego trajectory (good: realistic ambiguity). Low MM = model produces near-identical outputs (bad: over-deterministic).

H4 (frozen encoder, MM=4.0): The frozen encoder provides a fixed conditioning signal, leaving the denoiser's stochasticity intact. Each sample is genuinely different.

H6 (unfrozen encoder, MM=2.5): The joint training collapses the ego encoding into a near-deterministic lookup — same ego → almost same motion, every time.

Unfreezing the encoder trades stochastic generation for deterministic conditioning

This is a fundamental tension: the ego encoder learns to maximize R@1 by making the conditioning signal as discriminative as possible, but this also removes the ambiguity the diffusion model needs to generate diverse outputs. MM=2.5 (H6) vs MM=4.0 (H4) is a 37% drop. Whether this is acceptable depends on whether R@1 eventually surpasses H4 — a tradeoff that seg-2 will quantify.

Key Findings to Date
Finding 1: Cross-attention ego conditioning dramatically improves FID

H4 FID=3.392 vs H2 FID=6.963 — a 51% improvement. Per-timestep ego tokens allow the denoiser to align motion with specific moments in the trajectory, producing much more realistic motion overall. This finding is robust and consistent across CFG values and epochs.

Finding 2: Freezing the ego encoder creates a hard R@1 ceiling

H4 R@1 plateaus at 0.548 and never improves, even with 1800 more training epochs. The encoder was pretrained for mean-pooled alignment, not per-timestep discriminative features. This architectural mismatch is confirmed.

Finding 3: Unfreezing the encoder (H6) creates a FID/MM/R@1 three-way tradeoff

H6 shows improving R@1 but worsening FID and collapsing MM. The joint training causes encoder over-specialization — the conditioning signal becomes too deterministic, hurting both generation quality and diversity. Whether R@1 eventually surpasses the H4 ceiling (0.548) is the open question.

Finding 4: FID and R@1 do not co-optimize under joint training

In H4 (frozen), they coincidentally peaked at the same epoch (3399). In H6 (unfrozen), they explicitly diverge: best FID at ep=2099, best R@1 at ep=3299 (and still rising). This suggests fundamentally different loss landscapes for the two objectives and motivates separate optimization or auxiliary losses.

Finding 5: High H2 R@1 (0.767) is a retrieval artifact, not a quality signal

H2 conditions on a single mean-pooled token — a simpler retrieval target that produces high R@1 but poor FID (6.963). H4/H6 condition on 196 per-timestep tokens — harder retrieval but dramatically better generation. The R@1 metric must be interpreted relative to conditioning complexity.
