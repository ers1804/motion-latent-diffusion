⚠️ R@1 Ceiling — The Frozen Encoder Bottleneck
H4 R@1 ceiling: 0.548 — never approaches H2's 0.671

Training for 1800 MORE epochs (epoch 3399 → 4999) produced ZERO R@1 improvement. The ceiling is not a training data issue — it's architectural.

Root cause: FREEZE_EGO=True constrains the ego encoder

The pretrained ego encoder was optimized for contrastive alignment (mean-pooled ego → VAE latent). The cross-attention denoiser needs per-timestep discriminative features — a role the frozen encoder was never trained for and cannot adapt to.

H4 FID is excellent; R@1 is the unsolved problem

FID=3.392 proves the cross-attention architecture generates high-quality motion. The conditioning (R@1=0.548) is weaker because retrieval depends on ego encoder output quality, which is frozen to a suboptimal pretraining objective.

💡 Key Takeaways
Cross-attention ego conditioning (H4) dramatically outperforms pooled conditioning (H2) on FID

FID drops 48.6% (6.603 → 3.392). This is a strong, statistically significant result — non-overlapping confidence intervals. The per-timestep ego tokens enable the denoiser to align motion with specific moments in the ego trajectory.

Frozen ego encoder creates a hard R@1 ceiling at 0.548

The encoder was pretrained for mean-pooled alignment, not per-timestep cross-attention. 1800 additional training epochs cannot overcome this architectural constraint. H6 directly addresses this.

Early convergence: epoch=3399 is better than all later epochs

Unlike H2 (which improved through epoch 4399), H4 peaks at 3399 then degrades. Pattern may reflect the frozen encoder acting as a regularizer — without it (H6), training dynamics could differ.
